# Validated wildfire world and response-training architecture

## Context
Replace the course-project wildfire assumptions with terrain/weather-driven wildland spread, arbitrary georeferenced ignitions, explicit resource travel/work/refill, and response-policy training. Perfect reproduction is impossible with incomplete observations; success means reproducible physical models, measured errors, uncertainty, and honest validity gates. Scope: one California study area (existing Palisades bbox), noncommercial research, public data/local CPU-first compute, and wildland fire plus asset exposure—not building combustion or operational dispatch.

## Findings and review of the requested sequence
The inspected repository uses `FireSim -> InfernoEnv -> RelativeInfernoModel`. `src/env/grid_builder.py` constructs synthetic fuel and elevation-derived water; `src/env/fire_sim.py` uses per-tick neighbor probabilities. `src/env/inferno_env.py` models outbound travel but not return/refill, directly rewrites burning cells on water arrival, and exposes aggregate rather than per-unit state. Metadata specifies 30 m/EPSG:5070, contradicting the parent README's 200 m description. Generated GIS inputs are absent. Stored perimeter scores compare an unsuppressed simulation with a suppressed final perimeter and mostly frozen weather. They are not validation of spread physics or response effectiveness.

Build and verify the world before training policies against it, but do not wait for a fictitious perfect simulator. Maintain separate numerical, historical, suppression, and policy validation gates; training may exercise synthetic scenarios before historical validation, clearly labeled experimental. Existing station locations, GIS/routing patterns, PyTorch, and seeded rollouts are reusable; heuristic fuels, instant effects, zone-specific actions and checkpoints are not scientific ground truth.

## Approach
### 1. Introduce a versioned world contract and executable vertical slice
Add `src/world/` as the authoritative simulation package, with `contracts.py` defining scenario/ignition/resource/action/state types. Use UTC start times, elapsed seconds, projected metres, north-up raster transforms, explicit validity masks, and source provenance. Scenario directories contain `scenario.json`, aligned `landscape.npz`, hourly `weather.npz`, `roads.json`, and a resource roster in the manifest. No pickle loading. Define one validated public contract shared by fire, dispatch, training, evaluation and viewer; no direct policy mutation of simulation arrays.

The authoritative runtime is `World`, composed of fire and operations engines. A step accepts a batch of explicit unit actions, validates against current state, advances to the next bounded coupling instant, and returns observation and events. Keep numerical fire integration independent of decision intervals; canonical 60-second coupling boundaries ensure UI sampling does not alter stochastic behavior. Preserve burned-area history. Explicitly reject missing coverage, invalid ignition, unreachable travel and incompatible artifacts rather than silently fabricating geography/weather.

New modules are justified because the inspected code has no versioned geospatial scenario, physically dimensioned world clock, or per-unit operational state contract. Define these public signatures in `src/world/contracts.py` and consume them everywhere:
- `Scenario`: dataclass with `name: str`, `crs: str`, `transform: tuple[float, float, float, float, float, float]` in affine `(a,b,c,d,e,f)` order, `start_utc: str`, `duration_s: int`, `landscape: dict[str, np.ndarray]`, `weather: dict[str, np.ndarray]`, `weather_step_s: int`, `roads: dict`, `resources: list[dict]`, `provenance: dict`, `seed: int = 0`; method `validate() -> None` raises `ValueError` for invalid data. Require square north-up cells, projected metre units, identical landscape shapes, finite data within valid cells, and full time coverage. Invalid cells remain masked; never interpolate across them without marking the result invalid.
- Landscape keys: `elevation` (m), `slope` (rise/run), `aspect` (degrees clockwise from north), `fuel_model` (LANDFIRE/Scott–Burgan integer code), `canopy_cover` (fraction), `canopy_height` and `canopy_base_height` (m), `canopy_bulk_density` (kg/m³), `valid` (bool), `asset_value` (building-footprint area m² per cell, not dollar loss). Weather keys: `wind_speed_m_s`, `upwind_direction_deg`, `temperature_c`, `fuel_moisture_dead_1hr`, `fuel_moisture_dead_10hr`, `fuel_moisture_dead_100hr`, `fuel_moisture_live_herbaceous`, `fuel_moisture_live_woody`, `foliar_moisture`; moisture is kg/kg oven-dry fuel. Arrays have shape `(time,height,width)` or `(time,1,1)`; no implicit infinite last band. Store UTC validity interval for every band.
- `Action(unit_id: str | None = None, operation: str = 'wait', x: float | None = None, y: float | None = None)`: allowed operations `wait`, `water`, `line`, `return`. `water` and `line` require finite projected coordinates. Return uses the unit's configured base. Unknown units/operations or duplicate unit assignments in a batch raise `ValueError` before mutation.
- `Event(time_s: float, kind: str, unit_id: str | None, details: dict)` and `Treatment(x: float, y: float, radius_m: float, kind: str, strength: float, expires_s: float, time_s: float)`: JSON-serializable audit output; deterministic ordering by `(time_s, unit_id, kind)`.
- `load_scenario(path: Path) -> Scenario` and `save_scenario(scenario: Scenario, path: Path) -> None` in `src/world/scenario.py`; `scenario.json` has `schema_version: 1`, the scalar fields above, source hashes, and the unit roster; array paths resolve inside that directory only. Reject incompatible versions and overwrite unless CLI `--overwrite` was explicitly supplied. NPZ uses `allow_pickle=False`.
- Roads JSON: `nodes: [{id: str, x: float, y: float}]`, `edges: [{id: str, source: str, target: str, length_m: float, speed_m_s: float, geometry: [[x,y],...], closed: bool}]`; directed edges, positive lengths/speeds, source/target geometry consistency required. Preserve OSM one-way restrictions, explicit speed provenance and geometry; an imputed speed is labeled imputed, never measured.

### 2. Replace propagation with a maintained physics library
Use `pyretechnics==2026.10.20` through `src/world/fire.py`; the noncommercial license restriction was accepted. Reuse its surface/crown/spotting equations, `SpaceTimeCube`, `SpreadState`, and `spread_fire_with_phi_field`. Convert SI wind/time/slope at the adapter boundary. Use prescribed, timestamped dead/live fuel moisture, with provenance; do not substitute relative humidity for moisture. Fuel-moisture preprocessing may consume public NFDRS4 results; absent moisture is a data error, not permission to invent values for real scenarios.

Implement point ignition in longitude/latitude using pyproj and affine inversion, rejecting outside/nodata/nonburnable cells. Maintain spotting state across integration boundaries and distinct deterministic random substreams. Expose arrival time, burned footprint, spread intensity and frontier; never imply this landscape model solves coupled atmosphere or structure combustion. Suppression applies explicit, time-bounded spread modifiers and completed firelines; mark effectiveness parameters as experimental unless independently calibrated.

Implement `FireEngine(scenario: Scenario, seed: int)`, `ignite(longitude: float, latitude: float) -> None`, `advance(until_s: int, treatments: list[Treatment]) -> list[Event]`, and `snapshot() -> dict[str, np.ndarray]`. Preserve Pyretechnics arrival time and fire-type/intensity arrays; convert minutes to seconds and km/h to m/s only at the adapter. A cell is ignitable only if its supported fuel code is burnable and its validity mask is true. Multiple ignition calls before stepping are allowed; repeat ignition of an already burned cell has no additional effect and emits `ignition_ignored`. Do not clip invalid coordinates to an edge.

The inspected upstream implementation recreates its spotting RNG on each call. Do not assume returned RNG continuation is accepted on re-entry: derive a distinct seed using `numpy.random.SeedSequence([scenario_seed, coupling_index])` for each fixed 60-second call and carry returned pending `spot_ignitions` explicitly; pass fresh dictionaries, never rely on mutable default arguments. This defines reproducibility at the documented coupling resolution, not partition-invariant continuous randomness. Within each call, use upstream adaptive integration (`max_cells_per_timestep=0.4`). Emit `domain_exit` and censor area metrics when the frontier reaches an outer valid boundary. A partial final interval is allowed only at the scenario end; observation/view requests never call `advance`.

For experimental suppression, hold treatment modifiers constant within each 60-second interval and activate a completed work increment at the next boundary (maximum coupling delay 60 seconds, recorded in events). Water attenuates `fuel_spread_adjustment` in its delivered footprint with `exp(-k * dose_l_m2 * exp(-age_s / decay_s))`; `k`, `decay_s`, radius and application rate are required, provenance-tagged scenario parameters, not hidden universal constants. Completed line cells use zero spread adjustment until a recorded breach. Line progress consumes configured metres/second along a straight target segment from the unit's access point, rather than clearing a disk instantly. Ignition by spotting beyond a line remains possible. This does not simulate water thermodynamics, persistent smoldering, or guarantee containment; water/line results retain `suppression_validation: experimental` until independent measurements justify parameters. Never label zero frontier growth after one interval as permanent containment; report no-new-growth duration instead.

### 3. Make environmental inputs reproducible
Implement a local raster import/build command for explicit source files rather than hardcoded network fetches. Require DEM, LANDFIRE FBFM40 and canopy layers, aligned hourly wind/temperature/fuel-moisture arrays, road graph, and source manifests; reproject categorical rasters with nearest-neighbor and continuous rasters with bilinear resampling. Use EPSG:5070 and 30 m for the Palisades scenario. Valid water/nonburnable fuel classes remain distinct from nodata. Timestamp/version/checksum every source. Require pre-event fuels for historical hindcasts; do not use post-fire maps.

Retain existing public fetchers for acquiring DEM/roads/buildings; they are not authoritative fuel/weather preparation. Public sources: LANDFIRE LF2023 FBFM40 and canopy products, USGS 3DEP, HRRR hourly 3 km meteorology, and station observations as checks. Resampling HRRR is not terrain downscaling. Missing historical moisture/terrain winds remain explicit limitations; do not assert historical validation without them. Provide a deterministic synthetic scenario with known units for offline numerical and integration verification, distinctly labeled synthetic and never substituted for the real landscape.

Create `src/world/prepare.py` with `build --inputs INPUTS.json --out PATH`. INPUTS.json lists each source path, version, acquisition timestamp, original CRS/units, data-valid interval and checksum. Derive the target raster from a densified transformed envelope of bbox west=-118.605, south=34.030, east=-118.440, north=34.150; expand by a 3 km fire-domain buffer, retaining a separate core-study mask. Roads include a 20 km acquisition buffer so depots outside the fire grid are not snapped to its edge. A simulated perimeter reaching the fire-domain edge is censored, not contained.

Add `fetch_landfire.py` under `src/data_pipeline/`: download/cache official LF2023 FBFM40, canopy cover, canopy height, canopy base height and canopy bulk density packages, verify provider checksums where supplied, and extract/reproject only the needed region. Resolve product URLs from LANDFIRE's LF2023 catalog, not guessed filenames. A missing product is a named acquisition failure; allow the exact same product as a local file. Add `fetch_hrrr.py` for public NOAA archive GRIB2 files indexed by UTC date/hour: obtain 10 m U/V wind, 2 m temperature/RH, surface precipitation and shortwave radiation; retain native resolution and source valid times. Use HTTP range requests against the published GRIB index, with full-file download only when range access is unsupported. Use `cfgrib`/ecCodes to decode; no fabricated weather rows. Keep vectors through interpolation and derive meteorological wind-from direction with `atan2(-u,-v)` modulo 360. Use hourly analysis for hindcast truth; policies do not receive later analysis bands.

Add `src/world/moisture.py`: call the public NFDRS4 CLI, pinned to source commit `84223b95fd395a62ea344257f80a9949cee71e6e`, for each distinct native meteorological cell and vegetation configuration, supplying a full preceding year of hourly weather and documented station/elevation/latitude settings. Cache by inputs/config/binary hash and map dead/live outputs to the matching Pyretechnics fields; foliar moisture requires a separate provenance-tagged input because it is not interchangeable with herbaceous moisture. Use the upstream CLI's documented FW21/config formats after reading the pinned source. Where required history or live/foliar observations are absent, require an explicit interval prior in the scenario uncertainty manifest and label the result `moisture_source: assumed_prior`; sample that prior in ensembles and prohibit historical-readiness certification. Do not invent a single best-estimate moisture value silently.

Terrain winds are an uncertainty limitation in this implementation: use HRRR spatial fields, preserve their true resolution, and expose wind-speed/direction uncertainty intervals in the scenario manifest. Do not introduce an unvalidated custom slope-wind multiplier. A coupled/downscaled wind backend requires separate evidence before it can narrow these intervals.

### 4. Implement resource operations independently of policy
Add `src/world/resources.py`: explicit per-unit position, status, assignment, route progress, supply, remaining work, home base, and event timestamps. Unit types: engine, handcrew, helicopter. Actions: dispatch to a concrete point for water or line work, return to base, or wait. Ground units follow directed road-edge geometry and travel time from current position, with bounded last-mile access; helicopters use distance/airspeed plus mobilization and work/refill times. Unreachable goals are rejected. Unit selection is explicit; no resource-type teleportation or nearest-station proxies.

Lifecycle: available -> mobilizing -> traveling -> working -> returning -> refilling -> available; depleted units cannot perform further water work. Dispatch costs time before any effect. Work consumes supplies and elapsed time, and returns require travel. Bases/refill capacity, speed, work rate, mobilization and supply are scenario parameters with provenance. Synthetic values are labeled illustrative; no claim that public station rosters establish real availability. Closures and air-operation constraints must reject unsafe/unavailable actions; weather limits are configured research constraints, not operational authorization.

Define `ResourceEngine(scenario: Scenario)`, `validate_actions(actions: list[Action], time_s: float) -> None`, `submit(actions: list[Action], time_s: float) -> list[Event]`, `advance(until_s: float) -> tuple[list[Event], list[Treatment]]`, and `snapshot() -> list[dict]`. Unit records contain `id`, `kind`, `home_x`, `home_y`, `x`, `y`, `status`, `assignment`, `route`, `route_progress_m`, `supply_l`, `capacity_l`, `mobilization_s`, `speed_m_s`, `offroad_speed_m_s`, `max_access_m`, `work_duration_s`, `water_rate_l_s`, `line_rate_m_s`, `refill_rate_l_s`, `max_wind_m_s`, `shift_end_s`, and source labels for every operational parameter. Require type-appropriate fields, finite nonnegative rates and unique IDs. Handcrew supply is not water; engine/helicopter cannot execute `line`. Refilling occurs at home with one FIFO service slot per base by default; shared base IDs identify the queue. Repeated dispatch while busy is invalid. Only `return` may interrupt a task; return follows the current route position, never the last station.

Ground path cost is edge length divided by speed, capped by unit road speed; cache only by start node, goal node and graph-closure version. Engines may work only within their configured road-access distance; handcrews use a DEM-derived 8-neighbor access graph with travel cost `distance / (base_walk_speed * exp(-3.5 * abs(grade + 0.05)))`, forbid invalid/water cells and slopes above their configured limit. Explicitly label this walking-speed relation as an approximation. Helicopter cruise ground speed projects horizontal wind onto route direction, lower-bounded by a required positive configured limit; excessive wind or an unavailable air corridor rejects dispatch, not instant arrival. Weather/closure changes invalidate remaining routes at the next coupling boundary: halt travel at current position and emit `route_blocked` when no path exists. Unsafe work pauses, conserves supplies, and emits `work_suspended`; return is attempted only along a feasible path. No unit is teleported out of danger.

A step advances exactly 60 seconds or to the scenario end. Apply prior completed treatments, propagate fire, advance resource events, then queue newly completed treatments for the next boundary. Unit positions/events retain subinterval timing; report the bounded fire/operations coupling delay. Return `done=True` only at horizon or an explicit invalid-run terminal condition, never merely because a water drop stopped immediate growth.

### 6. Train and compare response policies using one contract
Add a masked candidate-action policy using PyTorch and the same feasible action generator for learned and rule-based policies. Score explicit unit/operation/target candidates; always include wait. No future realized weather, future fire truth, hidden engine parameters or mutable world objects in policy observations. Use PPO with fixed recorded hyperparameters; rewards use incremental burned area/asset exposure and resource time, not arbitrary extinguished-cell bonuses. Save schema/backend/scenario/seed provenance with checkpoints and reject incompatible files.

Unify no-response, nearest-feasible heuristic and learned-policy evaluation on identical scenario seeds. Separate training from evaluation seeds and historical incidents; report physical outcomes independently of reward. Do not deploy or label policies validated merely because their training reward improves. The old course models/checkpoints are historical artifacts, not compatible pretrained weights for the new world.

Place `candidate_actions(obs: dict, scenario: Scenario) -> list[Action]` in `src/world/actions.py`: wait first, then available units sorted by ID and at most 16 valid target locations sampled deterministically along the current frontier using equal arc-length spacing. Ground water targets are reachable road-access points nearest those locations; line targets are reachable unburned locations one cell ahead of the frontier; helicopters target the same observed frontier. Include return for busy units. Use exactly the resource engine's feasibility rules for masks and actual dispatch; generator inputs must not include future world state. During learning, sample one action per decision interval; the world API still supports batches for manual dispatch.

Replace course-required auxiliary classification with a 2-layer 64-unit tanh MLP over candidate features (operation, normalized unit position/supply/status, target position/current fire intensity/asset exposure, feasible ETA), masked categorical actor and a separate 2-layer 64-unit pooled-observation critic. This intentionally starts with an inspectable small policy rather than claiming a pretrained CNN is necessary. Implement PPO in `src/world/learning.py`: Adam learning rate 3e-4, gamma .99, GAE lambda .95, clip .2, value coefficient .5, entropy coefficient .01, gradient norm .5, 256 transitions/update, 4 epochs, minibatch 64, seed recorded. Normalize features with scenario bounds and configured capacities, not evaluation statistics. Default reward per step is `-(new_burned_m2 / valid_burnable_m2) - (new_exposed_asset_m2 / max(total_asset_m2,1)) - 0.01 * busy_unit_seconds / max(unit_count * duration_s,1)`; these are explicit research utility weights, not economic damage estimates. Ignore already-exposed assets in incremental penalties.

`evaluate` uses seeds 10000–10031 by default, never training seeds (default 0–999), and reports paired deltas against no-response and nearest-feasible earliest-arrival dispatch. Checkpoint metadata includes schema version, engine version, model feature ordering, scenario/input hashes, training seeds and hyperparameters. Load state dictionaries with safe tensor-only loading; reject incompatible metadata rather than truncate tensors or warm-start old checkpoints.

Clean cutover: replace active `src/env/fire_sim.py`, `src/env/inferno_env.py`, `src/env/grid_builder.py` and legacy active training/model entry points with the new world path; do not maintain compatibility aliases or two supported simulators. Before removal, inventory all consumers with `grep` pattern `from (src\.)?env\.|import (src\.)?env|FireSim\(|InfernoEnv\(|RelativeInfernoModel|/get_latest_action` under `InfernoTactics`, including `experiments/`, notebooks and tests. Migrate useful behavioral tests and active examples; remove obsolete zone/classification/training experiments and their now-unused model classes, since they encode the replaced simulator. Preserve historical logs/checkpoint binaries as clearly unsupported research artifacts, not executable entry points. Verify zero active imports of removed symbols. Perform each behavior cutover with its consumers in the same change, after the new path has a working smoke scenario.

Check matching UTC times, CRS, masks and observation provenance. Do not turn missing perimeters into zero-error results. Identify suppression-contaminated observations and prevent them from certifying free-spread physics. The historical gate remains unmet until independently held-out, time-resolved incident data and an externally justified acceptance protocol are supplied; a code implementation cannot guarantee this outcome.

Implement `src/world/validation.py` with `score_perimeters(predicted: list[dict], observed: list[dict], scenario: Scenario) -> dict` and `evaluate_ensemble(scenario: Scenario, seeds: list[int], policy: str) -> dict`. Observations require `observed_at_utc`, geometry CRS, source, spatial uncertainty, and `suppression_status: known_absent|known_present|unknown`. Match timestamps exactly or evaluate simulation at the observation time; never silently substitute a final perimeter or a bare date. Rasterize both on the common valid domain; report IoU, area ratio (null with reason when observed area is zero), symmetric mean boundary distance and 95th-percentile boundary distance in metres. Score ensembles with cellwise Brier score and interval coverage only where observation support exists. Record source resolution: VIIRS-derived 375 m/12-hour products cannot prove 30 m/minute accuracy. Initial seeded uncertainty ensembles use scenario-specified wind/moisture/suppression intervals and independent deterministic RNG streams; absent intervals prevent a calibrated-probability claim.

Readiness report fields: `numerical: pass|fail`, `operations: pass|fail`, `historical: unvalidated|evaluated|validated`, `suppression: experimental|validated`, `policy: experimental|evaluated`, plus evidence IDs and reasons. `validated` requires at least three independent incidents with time-resolved observations, disjoint incident-level calibration/validation/test splits, matching environmental coverage, and pre-registered thresholds from a domain-reviewed protocol. The Palisades-only initial study cannot satisfy this gate; report it as unvalidated/evaluated without expanding geography or inventing incidents. Freeze thresholds before inspecting test outcomes. Until those external data/criteria exist, software verification and policy experiments can complete, but the user's desired accurate-real-world model cannot honestly be declared complete.
